A Working Model

The Practice Tree

How a litigation practice can catalog its craft, its law, and its judgment, so the same standard reaches every matter, and the right guidance is already in front of you for the task at hand.

Every matter draws on the same accumulated know-how: the disciplines that govern any document, the craft of filing and briefing and taking a deposition, the substantive law for each kind of case, and the individual attorney’s own voice. Most of that lives in people’s heads and gets reapplied from memory, one file at a time. This is a way to catalog it instead, as a living tree.

It is written so that an assistant the practice uses, whether a new associate or an AI tool, can apply each piece directly, instead of reassembling it by hand on every matter.

Roots below the surface, the filter
The disciplines, and the safeguard

The roots are not a passive foundation. They are the tree’s filtration system. The tree is fed by watering the soil, and every task, instruction, record, and tool output is an input; the roots are selective about what they take in, absorbing what is sound and rejecting what is not, so that poison in the soil never travels up into the trunk, the branches, or the leaves. This is where the universal disciplines live, verifying every legal proposition against its source, writing clearly, and billing soundly, and it is where every input is screened before any work is built on it.

Four gates run on every input: truth, verify against the source or hold; propriety, clear the professional-responsibility screens or refuse; necessity, cut anything the matter does not need; and integrity, flag the predictable corruption in tool and dictation inputs.

Why catalog it this way

How it works in practice

The value is not the diagram. It is what the structure makes possible on a real file.

i

The right guidance, already assembled

Sit down to draft an answer and the relevant pieces arrive together: the answer’s checklist, the defenses that fit the case, the filing format, the verification discipline, and your own voice, instead of you gathering them by hand from memory and old files.

ii

One standard, every matter

Everyone working from the same tree produces work to the same standard. The only thing that varies from one attorney to the next is the soil, the individual’s voice and habits.

iii

The law stays honest

Every substantive point is flagged to be confirmed against the primary source. Nothing important rests on recollection, which is where citation errors and stale law creep in.

iv

Change once, not fifty times

When a statute changes, you update the one branch that carries it, not every document that ever relied on it. When you refine how you write, you update the soil, and it flows upward into everything.

v

Onboarding in days, not years

A new associate inherits the accumulated craft and law at once, instead of absorbing it case by case over years. The tree is the institutional memory, written down and kept current.

The documents are the leaves. What makes them good is everything underneath, ending in the soil, which is yours.

The whole canopy, named
