Legal research and verification method

A drafting workflow built around primary-source verification. Platform-portable: Lexis+ as it runs today, CourtListener for native retrieval. The two verification gates do not move regardless of which platform supplies the text.

Devin R. Horowitz · prepared for internal collaboration
Operating principle. A verification instruction only works when it is backed by primary-source retrieval. A model told to use primary sources with no way to fetch them will lean on secondary text, or narrate a verification it never performed. That is the failure that lets a brief misstate whether one case overruled another. The method closes it with two gates: every authority is checked against the verbatim opinion before it is used, and every load-bearing authority clears a citator check before it enters a filing.

The method, step by step

Tool tags mark who does the work: attorney judgment, Claude or CourtListener, or a Lexis+ touchpoint. The two gates are where verification actually happens.

  1. Frame the legal issue. State the precise question for the matter before any searching. The framing controls what counts as on point later.
  2. Generate the searches. Claude Produce a semicolon-separated citation list and a separate set of boolean queries. Citations are for direct paste; queries are for finding additional authority.
  3. Retrieve verbatim opinions. Claude Pull the full text of each opinion. Today this is a manual Lexis+ pull; under the CourtListener port the search runs natively and the opinion text comes back without a manual step.
  4. Gate one — verbatim verification. gate Read the actual opinion and confirm it holds what the proposition claims. Never rely on headnotes, treatises, or party summaries. On any mismatch, discard or re-read. This is the gate the secondary-source error defeats.
  5. Relevance triage. Keep only authority that is dispositive or on point. Do not include a case because the search surfaced it; set marginal authority aside.
  6. Gate two — citator check. gate Lexis+ Confirm the authority is still good law in Lexis+ (Shepard's) before any brief-level reliance. A citation graph is a useful first pass but is not a substitute for the citator.
  7. Curate the case corpus. Only verified, on-point authority enters the corpus that goes to drafting. The corpus is the chosen set, not everything found.
  8. Draft with the corpus. Claude The model drafts from the curated corpus. Every citation is flagged unverified; nothing is asserted as good law on the model's word.
  9. Adversarial review (optional). Claude Run an opposing-counsel pass and a judicial-first-impression pass over the draft to surface weak points before anyone else does.
  10. Final authentication. Lexis+ Re-confirm each load-bearing citation before filing. Nothing load-bearing reaches a filing unverified.

Porting retrieval to CourtListener

  • Retrieval is reassigned, not redesigned. Step three's manual keyword search and pull moves to Claude running the search natively on CourtListener and reading the verbatim opinion. The role shifts from puller to reviewer; everything else holds.
  • Gate one is what CourtListener most improves. Because the model now reads the actual opinion text instead of secondary sources, verbatim verification gets stronger. That is the fix for the case-was-misread problem, and it is the whole reason to add the connection.
  • Gate two stays on Lexis+. Keep the citator check before anything load-bearing goes in a filing. CourtListener's treatment flagging is a useful first pass, not Shepard's-grade. The result is a hybrid: CourtListener for fast native retrieval and verbatim verification, Lexis+ for citator validation. Not a swap.
