Liberty Surplus Insurance Corporation v. Kaufman Lynn Construction, Inc.

// U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit · decided Mar 5, 2025
Liberty Surplus Insurance Corporation v. Kaufman Lynn Construction, Inc.
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit · decided Mar 5, 2025 · Nos. 23-12715 & 23-12835 · affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded
coverageprocedure

Applying Florida law, the Eleventh Circuit construed a Course of Construction Exclusion in a commercial general liability policy, which barred coverage for property damage to any insured project during the course of construction until the project is completed. The court held that the undefined phrase until the project is completed unambiguously means the entire multi-phase project must be finished, not merely the portion that sustained damage, so water damage to completed buildings was excluded while other buildings remained under construction and old buildings remained to be demolished. The court declined to import the policy's products-completed operations hazard definition of completed work into the exclusion, reasoning that your work and project are not synonymous. The coverage ruling was affirmed.

Why it matters: It establishes how courts applying Florida law read a Course of Construction Exclusion in a CGL policy, holding that completion of the project means the entire project rather than the damaged component.

coverageprocedure

The court held that an insured has Article III standing to pursue a reformation counterclaim based on mutual mistake even without a pending claim on the disputed portion of the policy. Assuming the reformation claim would succeed, the insured received a policy different from the one it bargained for and paid a premium not commensurate with the coverage provided, which is a concrete and particularized benefit-of-the-bargain economic injury, and the claim presents no ripeness problem because it rests on a past mutual mistake rather than contingent future events. The court reversed the dismissal of the reformation counterclaim and remanded.

Why it matters: It clarifies that a reformation claim for an insurance policy may proceed in federal court before any claim is made on the disputed coverage, treating the discrepancy between the bargained-for and issued policy as a present injury.

procedure

The court held that the insurer could not recover attorney's fees under Florida's offer-of-judgment statute, section 768.79, and Rule 1.442 because its settlement proposal failed to resolve all claims between the parties. By treating the insured's declaratory and breach-of-contract counterclaims as essentially monetary while omitting from the proposal its own mirror-image declaratory-judgment claim regarding coverage, the insurer failed to comply with the requirement that a proposal resolve all claims for monetary relief. The denial of fees was affirmed.

Why it matters: It clarifies that under section 768.79 and Rule 1.442 a settlement proposal must resolve all claims between the parties, so a proponent that characterizes the opponent's declaratory claim as monetary cannot omit its own mirror-image declaratory claim from the proposal.
