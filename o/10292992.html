Lubin v. Starbucks Corporation

Lubin v. Starbucks Corporation
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit · decided Dec 16, 2024 · No. 21-11215 · affirmed
procedure

A former employee's spouse, who obtained health coverage as a dependent but never signed an arbitration agreement, brought a putative class action against the employer alleging deficient COBRA notices. The employer moved to compel arbitration under the wife's employment agreement. The Eleventh Circuit held that a non-signatory cannot be compelled to arbitrate under an agreement he did not sign, particularly where his claim arises from an independent federal statutory right rather than from the contract. The court also held that the agreement's delegation clause did not clearly and unmistakably commit arbitrability to the arbitrator because it conflicted with an exclusion clause carving out actions to compel arbitration, so the court, not the arbitrator, decided arbitrability.

Why it matters: It clarifies that an arbitration agreement's delegation clause does not provide the clear and unmistakable evidence needed to send arbitrability to the arbitrator when it conflicts with an exclusion clause for actions to compel arbitration, and that a non-signatory suing on an independent statutory right is not bound to arbitrate.

procedure

The court applied Florida contract law to the employer's three theories for binding a non-signatory. It held that equitable estoppel did not apply because the non-signatory was not relying on or seeking the benefits of the wife's employment agreement, the third-party beneficiary doctrine did not apply because that doctrine binds a beneficiary only when he sues to enforce the contract, and the derivative-claim theory failed because the spouse's right to a COBRA notice under 29 U.S.C. section 1166(a)(4) is an independent statutory right rather than one derivative of the participant's right.

Why it matters: It clarifies the limits of the equitable estoppel, third-party beneficiary, and derivative-claim exceptions used to bind non-signatories to arbitration agreements under state contract law.
