ECB USA, Inc. v. Chubb Insurance Company of New Jersey

// U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit · decided Aug 29, 2024
ECB USA, Inc. v. Chubb Insurance Company of New Jersey
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit · decided Aug 29, 2024 · No. 22-10811 · affirmed
coverageprocedure

An accounting firm's professional liability policy covered liability arising from management consulting services, defined as services directed toward expertise in banking finance, accounting, risk and systems analysis, design and implementation, asset recovery and strategy planning for financial institutions. The insured performed an allegedly negligent audit for a food company, and the assignees of the insured's rights sued the insurer for coverage. Applying New Jersey law and the series-qualifier canon, the court held that the postpositive phrase 'for financial institutions' modifies every parallel item in the list, including 'accounting,' so an audit performed for a non-financial-institution client was not covered. The court rejected the assignees' reliance on the last-antecedent and nearest-reasonable-referent canons, reasoning those canons do not apply to a parallel series of nouns.

Why it matters: The decision applies the series-qualifier canon to construe a scope-of-coverage definition, holding that an end-of-list qualifier modifies every parallel item in the list rather than only the last, which guides interpretation of similarly structured coverage grants.

coverage

The court held that the contra proferentem doctrine did not apply for two independent reasons under New Jersey law. First, the policy was not genuinely ambiguous because one reading was superior to the other, and the doctrine is one of last resort available only when a court cannot otherwise determine the contract's meaning. Second, even if there were a genuine interpretive dispute, contra proferentem does not apply where both parties are sophisticated commercial entities with equal bargaining power, and the insured accounting firm qualified as such.

Why it matters: The holding clarifies that contra proferentem is unavailable when a policy is unambiguous and, independently, when the insured is a sophisticated commercial entity, limiting when ambiguities are construed against the insurer.

procedure

The court held that a party may raise canons of construction for the first time on appeal to support an interpretation it advanced below, distinguishing between issues or positions, which can be waived or forfeited through litigation conduct, and authorities or arguments, which cannot. It reasoned that a canon is merely additional authority for the same position and cannot be forfeited any more than the existence of a precedent or the words of a statute. The court also held that a district court's denial of a motion for reconsideration does not change the standard of review applied to the underlying judgment, and that appellate waiver is assessed de novo regardless of the district court's waiver determination.

Why it matters: The decision clarifies that canons of construction are arguments rather than issues for appellate-waiver purposes and that the standard of review on the underlying disposition is unaffected by an unchallenged denial of reconsideration.
