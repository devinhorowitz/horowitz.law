The appellate watch reads decisions; this reads statutes. It watches the Georgia General Assembly for bills that became law — signed by the Governor, or allowed to become law without signature — and vetoes, and drafts a plain-English card for the ones that touch a civil-litigation and insurance-defense practice: tort reform, damages, civil procedure, evidence, insurance, premises, and motor-carrier law. It also carries the rare federal statute that reaches a Georgia practice — FAAAA motor-carrier preemption, the FMCSA's mandate, federal jurisdiction — the same core-plus-federal shape the case feed has.

Unlike the case feed, every entry here is confirmed by a person before it appears — enactments are rare and consequential, so nothing publishes on the machine alone. Each summary is AI-drafted; the enrolled bill is the authority. The case feed lives at the watch.

EnactedGa.HB295May 12, 2026
Local government; procedures for real property owners to make claims for compensation for loss of property value or expenses incurred; provisions
procedurepremisesdamages

HB295 amends Chapter 60 of Title 36 (general provisions for counties and municipalities) to create a statutory procedure by which real property owners may present claims to a local government for lost property value or expenses caused by that government's failure to comply with or enforce specified laws, ordinances, and resolutions, or by the local government's maintenance of a public nuisance. Previously, such claims against counties and cities proceeded under general tort, nuisance, and inverse condemnation theories subject to sovereign and official immunity defenses and generally applicable ante litem notice rules, with no dedicated compensation-claim mechanism in Chapter 60. The provided title and description do not specify the claim filing deadlines, notice content, response obligations, damages measures or caps, or whether and when suit may follow an unpaid claim, so those details must be confirmed from the enrolled text.

Why it matters: Creates a new administrative claim path against Georgia local governments for property-value losses from nonenforcement or public nuisance, adding a pre-suit step to counsel.

EnactedGa.HB185May 11, 2026
Professions and businesses; repeal and reenact Chapter 11A, the Dietetics Practice Act
expertprocedure

HB185 repeals and reenacts OCGA Title 43, Chapter 11A, the Dietetics Practice Act, and makes conforming cross-reference amendments in Titles 9 (civil practice), 31, 33 (insurance), 43, and 51 (torts). The description identifies the Title 9, 33, and 51 changes only as conforming cross-references, so the substantive civil-practice rules appear unchanged; the provided text does not identify which specific code sections (for example, the professional malpractice affidavit or expert competency provisions that enumerate licensed dietitians) are being renumbered or relabeled. Practitioners relying on statutory references to dietitians or the prior chapter should verify current section numbers and any changed licensure terminology in the reenacted chapter.

Why it matters: Renumbered dietetics licensure references may alter citations in malpractice affidavit, expert competency, and insurance statutes without changing substantive civil rules.

EnactedGa.HB339May 14, 2025
Motor vehicles; exempt ride share drivers or ride share network services from definition of motor carrier
autocoverage

HB339 amends Georgia's motor carrier article (Title 40, Chapter 1, Article 3) to expressly exclude ride share drivers and ride share network services from the definition of "motor carrier," adds definitions, and revises the article's legislative findings. It also provides that ride share network services are not liable for the actions of ride share drivers in certain circumstances, and makes conforming changes elsewhere in the article. Because the motor carrier designation is the gateway to the regulatory and insurance-related obligations imposed by that article, drivers and network services operating under this exemption are no longer treated as motor carriers for those purposes. The bill text as described does not spell out which circumstances trigger the liability exemption or how it interacts with any other basis for holding a network service liable, so those limits must be read from the enacted language itself.

Why it matters: Ride share drivers and networks fall outside motor carrier status, narrowing claims and insurer-joinder theories that depend on that designation.

EnactedGa.HB816May 13, 2025
Greene County Public Facilities Authority Act; enact
procedure

This local act creates the Greene County Public Facilities Authority, an entity that did not previously exist, and sets out its membership, powers, purposes, and authority to issue revenue bonds. Of direct relevance to litigators, the act includes provisions on tort immunity for the authority and fixes the venue and jurisdiction of actions involving it, along with provisions on moneys received and trust funds and tax exemption. Counsel handling claims arising from facilities or projects in Greene County should confirm whether the authority is the correct defendant and how its immunity and venue provisions apply before filing.

Why it matters: Adds a Greene County public authority defendant with statutory tort immunity and fixed venue and jurisdiction for actions against it.

EnactedGa.HB625May 13, 2025
Fulton County; State Court; provide additional judge
procedure

This local Act amends the 1976 consolidation Act creating the State Court of Fulton County (as amended in 1994 and 2003) to add two judgeships to that court, increasing its bench to twelve judges. The two additional judges are to be appointed by the Governor as soon as practicable after the Act's effective date, for terms beginning on the date of appointment and expiring December 31, 2026, and until successors are elected and qualified; thereafter the seats are filled by election. The additional judges have the same powers and privileges as the other judges of the court, and provision is made for compensation and expenses.

Why it matters: Fulton County State Court gains two judges, bringing its bench to twelve and adding civil trial capacity in a high-volume venue for tort and insurance-defense cases.

EnactedGa.HB114May 8, 2025
Motor vehicles; federal regulations regarding safe operation of commercial motor vehicle and carriers; update reference date
autonegligent security

HB114 amends O.C.G.A. § 40-1-8 to update the reference date for incorporating federal safety regulations governing motor carriers, commercial motor vehicles, drivers, and hazardous-materials transport, and revises a definition within that section. The effect is to align Georgia's adopted federal safety standards with a more current version of the federal regulations. The bill text does not specify the new reference date or which definition is revised.

Why it matters: Updated federal safety standards now apply as the negligence-per-se benchmark in commercial motor vehicle and trucking cases.

EnactedGa.HB94May 1, 2025
Insurance; medically necessary expenses for standard fertility preservation services when a medically necessary treatment for cancer, sickle cell disease, or lupus may directly or indirectly cause an impairment of fertility; require coverage
coverage

Amends Chapter 24 of Title 33 to require certain insurance policies to cover medically necessary expenses for standard fertility preservation services when a medically necessary treatment for cancer, sickle cell disease, or lupus may directly or indirectly impair fertility. The Act provides definitions, exclusions, permissible cost-sharing, and authorizes implementing rules and regulations. The prior rule did not mandate this specific coverage; the text does not spell out the full scope of affected policies here.

Why it matters: Expands mandated insurance coverage, potentially generating coverage disputes over fertility-preservation benefits tied to cancer, sickle cell, or lupus treatment.

EnactedGa.HB179Apr 30, 2025
Appellate practice; digital recording of certain trial proceedings; provide
procedure

HB 179 amends the appellate practice provisions of O.C.G.A. Title 5, Chapter 6, Article 2, along with Title 9, Article 2 of Chapter 14 (post-conviction procedure) and Title 15 (courts, including the Georgia State-wide Business Court), to authorize digital recording of specified trial proceedings and to set rules governing the use and regulation of digital recording systems, with accompanying definitions. Previously, the statutory framework for making the record in these proceedings did not expressly provide for digital recording as an authorized method; the Act now supplies that authorization and its parameters. The description does not identify which specific proceedings may be digitally recorded, what certification or transcription requirements apply, or how a digital recording is to be reduced to a transcript for the record on appeal, so those details must be read in the enacted text.

Why it matters: Civil litigators may face digitally recorded proceedings, including in the Business Court, changing how the record and appellate transcript are created and verified.

EnactedGa.HB268Apr 28, 2025
Education; safety, health, and well-being of students and school communities; provide
negligent securityprocedure

HB 268 amends Chapter 2 of Title 20 to create an Office of Safe Schools within GEMA/HS, to require technical assistance for local school systems in developing threat management team policies and procedures, and to establish a state-wide behavioral threat management operational process with accompanying definitions. It also amends O.C.G.A. 16-11-37 to criminalize threatening death or serious injury to persons who are or are likely to be at school, and amends Article 4 of Chapter 18 of Title 50 to exempt certain records from public inspection. The text supplied does not identify which specific records fall within the Open Records exemption, nor does it state any immunity provisions for schools or threat management team members.

Why it matters: Creates statutory school threat-assessment duties relevant to negligent security claims while shielding some related records from Open Records requests.

Ethics opinions

Formal Advisory Opinions of the State Bar of Georgia, on the Georgia Rules of Professional Conduct. The Formal Advisory Opinion Board issues them, they are filed with the Supreme Court of Georgia, and once the Court acts they bind every Georgia lawyer. They are not published as case law and appear in no court-opinion database, so this is the only place the watch can catch them. Extracted from gabar.org; confirmed by hand.
