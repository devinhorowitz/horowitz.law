The appellate watch reads decisions; this reads statutes. It watches the Georgia General Assembly for bills that became law — signed by the Governor, or allowed to become law without signature — and vetoes, and drafts a plain-English card for the ones that touch a civil-litigation and insurance-defense practice: tort reform, damages, civil procedure, evidence, insurance, premises, and motor-carrier law. It also carries the rare federal statute that reaches a Georgia practice — FAAAA motor-carrier preemption, the FMCSA's mandate, federal jurisdiction — the same core-plus-federal shape the case feed has.

Unlike the case feed, every entry here is confirmed by a person before it appears — enactments are rare and consequential, so nothing publishes on the machine alone. Each summary is AI-drafted; the enrolled bill is the authority. The case feed lives at the watch.

EnactedGa.HB114May 8, 2025
Motor vehicles; federal regulations regarding safe operation of commercial motor vehicle and carriers; update reference date
autonegligent security

HB114 amends O.C.G.A. § 40-1-8 to update the reference date for incorporating federal safety regulations governing motor carriers, commercial motor vehicles, drivers, and hazardous-materials transport, and revises a definition within that section. The effect is to align Georgia's adopted federal safety standards with a more current version of the federal regulations. The bill text does not specify the new reference date or which definition is revised.

Why it matters: Updated federal safety standards now apply as the negligence-per-se benchmark in commercial motor vehicle and trucking cases.

EnactedGa.HB94May 1, 2025
Insurance; medically necessary expenses for standard fertility preservation services when a medically necessary treatment for cancer, sickle cell disease, or lupus may directly or indirectly cause an impairment of fertility; require coverage
coverage

Amends Chapter 24 of Title 33 to require certain insurance policies to cover medically necessary expenses for standard fertility preservation services when a medically necessary treatment for cancer, sickle cell disease, or lupus may directly or indirectly impair fertility. The Act provides definitions, exclusions, permissible cost-sharing, and authorizes implementing rules and regulations. The prior rule did not mandate this specific coverage; the text does not spell out the full scope of affected policies here.

Why it matters: Expands mandated insurance coverage, potentially generating coverage disputes over fertility-preservation benefits tied to cancer, sickle cell, or lupus treatment.

Federal regulations

Agency rulemaking that reaches a Georgia trucking practice — chiefly the FMCSA’s final rules in 49 CFR 350–399: hours of service, driver qualification, vehicle maintenance, and the financial-responsibility (insurance) minimums. A new safety standard can anchor a negligence-per-se theory; a coverage-minimum change moves the money. From the Federal Register. RSS.

Court rules

Amendments to the Federal Rules — Civil Procedure, Evidence, and Appellate Procedure. These are neither statutes nor agency rules: they change through the Rules Enabling Act and take effect December 1. A new Rule 26 or Rule 702 reshapes discovery or expert practice the day it lands.
