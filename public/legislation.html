Georgia Legislative Watch

// law that moved, not just cases

The appellate watch reads decisions; this reads statutes. It watches the Georgia General Assembly for bills that became law — signed by the Governor, or allowed to become law without signature — and vetoes, and drafts a plain-English card for the ones that touch a civil-litigation and insurance-defense practice: tort reform, damages, civil procedure, evidence, insurance, premises, and motor-carrier law. It also carries the rare federal statute that reaches a Georgia practice — FAAAA motor-carrier preemption, the FMCSA's mandate, federal jurisdiction — the same core-plus-federal shape the case feed has.

Unlike the case feed, every entry here is confirmed by a person before it appears — enactments are rare and consequential, so nothing publishes on the machine alone. Each summary is AI-drafted; the enrolled bill is the authority. The case feed lives at the watch.

Federal regulations

Agency rulemaking that reaches a Georgia trucking practice — chiefly the FMCSA’s final rules in 49 CFR 350–399: hours of service, driver qualification, vehicle maintenance, and the financial-responsibility (insurance) minimums. A new safety standard can anchor a negligence-per-se theory; a coverage-minimum change moves the money. From the Federal Register. RSS.

Court rules

Amendments to the Federal Rules — Civil Procedure, Evidence, and Appellate Procedure. These are neither statutes nor agency rules: they change through the Rules Enabling Act and take effect December 1. A new Rule 26 or Rule 702 reshapes discovery or expert practice the day it lands. Extracted from uscourts.gov; the lightest-touch of the three watches, since the rules move once a year.
