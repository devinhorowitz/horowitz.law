Wasserman v. Franklin County

// Supreme Court of Georgia · decided Jan 28, 2025
Wasserman v. Franklin County
Supreme Court of Georgia · decided Jan 28, 2025 · No. S23G1029 · vacated and remanded
procedure

Plaintiff sought to assert a third party's federal equal protection rights in Georgia courts under the federal doctrine of third-party standing. The Georgia Supreme Court held that the federal doctrine of third-party standing is not part of Georgia constitutional standing law, because the Judicial Power Paragraph of the Georgia Constitution requires a plaintiff, at a minimum, to assert a violation of her own legal rights to invoke a Georgia court's judicial power, and overruled Burgess and later decisions to the extent they adopted that doctrine. The Court also rejected the plaintiff's Supremacy Clause argument, holding that Georgia's constitutional standing requirement is a neutral jurisdictional rule that may be applied to bar a Section 1983 claim brought by someone asserting only a nonparty's rights. The Court vacated and remanded, leaving the plaintiff's separate assignment-of-claim theory for the courts below to address in the first instance.

Why it matters: It establishes that a Georgia plaintiff must assert a violation of her own legal rights to have standing and eliminates federal third-party standing as a basis for suing in Georgia courts, a threshold rule of broad importance to all civil litigators.

procedure

The plaintiff argued that applying Georgia's standing requirements to bar her federal Section 1983 claim would violate the Supremacy Clause. The Court held that Georgia's constitutional standing rule is a neutral jurisdictional rule that does not target or discriminate against federal causes of action, and so may be applied evenhandedly to bar a Section 1983 claim asserted by a plaintiff who alleges only a nonparty's rights.

Why it matters: It clarifies that Georgia's constitutional standing requirement is a neutral jurisdictional rule enforceable against federal claims in state court without offending the Supremacy Clause.
