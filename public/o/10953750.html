Jackson v. Cantrell

// Court of Appeals of Georgia · decided Aug 19, 2026
Jackson v. Cantrell
Court of Appeals of Georgia, Fourth Division · decided Aug 19, 2026 · No. A26A1027 · reversed
coverageprocedureautodamages

Plaintiff was shot accidentally by a 15-year-old passenger inside a truck owned by the teen's father, and after suit he signed a limited release under OCGA 33-24-41.1 that released the settling insurer and the individual defendants except to the extent other insurance coverage is available covering his claims. Applying Harvey v. Hall, the court held that a defendant moving for summary judgment on the release as an affirmative defense bears the initial burden of establishing the prima facie defense, including that no other coverage applies, before the burden shifts to the plaintiff. The court noted that affirmative evidence of coverage denial is unnecessary for a policy plainly inapplicable on its face, but here the movants did not address the commercial auto policy's non-owned auto provision covering vehicles owned by LLC members used in the business or personal affairs, or its permissive-user definition of insured, so they failed to carry that initial burden. The court expressly did not decide whether coverage actually exists, and reversed the grant of summary judgment.

Why it matters: It clarifies how the burden-shifting framework for a limited-release affirmative defense operates when other policies exist, requiring the movant to account for the policy's own coverage-granting provisions rather than relying on scheduled vehicles and drivers alone.

procedure

The defendants argued the appeal should be affirmed because the appellant did not designate the entire record for transmission. The court rejected that contention, holding that because it may consider the record from a prior appeal in the same case, an appellant need not designate for inclusion parts of the record already before the court from that earlier appeal. The court also observed that nothing necessary for review was missing.

Why it matters: It confirms that a notice of appeal limited to materials filed since a prior appeal in the same case is not a fatal record defect.

procedure

The appellant contended the trial court erred by ignoring an amended complaint seeking a declaratory judgment that the LLC's commercial auto policy provided coverage. The court declined to address the amended complaint's propriety in the first instance because it sits to correct errors of law made by trial courts. It further noted that its prior affirmance of summary judgment for the LLC is the law of the case and binding in subsequent proceedings, so the LLC is no longer a party.

Why it matters: It reiterates that an appellate court will not rule on a matter the trial court never addressed and that an earlier affirmance of summary judgment for a party removes that party from the case as law of the case.

damagesprocedure

The trial court had awarded attorney fees to defense counsel under OCGA 9-11-68 based on its grant of summary judgment. Because that award depended on the summary judgment ruling the court reversed, the court also reversed the fee award.

Why it matters: It confirms that an OCGA 9-11-68 fee award falls with the underlying judgment that supported it.
