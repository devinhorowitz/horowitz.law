Hensley v. Westin Hotel
U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit · decided Sep 4, 2024 · No. 22-11458 · vacated and remanded with instructions to remand to state court
procedurepremises

A worker injured in a trip and fall at a hotel sued in Georgia state court, and the defendants removed based on diversity jurisdiction. A workers' compensation insurer, Hartford, intervened to protect its subrogation lien under O.C.G.A. section 34-9-11.1 and filed an intervenor complaint against a defendant that shared its Connecticut citizenship. The Eleventh Circuit held that Hartford had a right to intervene under Rule 24(a)(2) because Georgia law requires such insurers to intervene to protect their subrogation rights, and that Hartford was an indispensable party under Rule 19(b) because a judgment in its absence would impair its lien and no remedy could mitigate that prejudice. Because Hartford's intervention destroyed complete diversity and section 1367(b) barred supplemental jurisdiction over its claims, the district court lost subject matter jurisdiction once intervention was granted.

Why it matters: The decision establishes that a workers' compensation subrogation insurer intervening under O.C.G.A. section 34-9-11.1 is both an intervenor of right and an indispensable party whose nondiverse citizenship destroys diversity jurisdiction, requiring vacatur and remand to state court rather than dismissal of the intervenor.
