Fidler v. AIKG, LLC

// Court of Appeals of Georgia · decided Sep 29, 2026
Fidler v. AIKG, LLC
Court of Appeals of Georgia, Third Division · decided Sep 29, 2026 · Nos. A26A1558 & A26A1559 · vacated and remanded in the main appeal; affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded in the cross appeal
procedure

Plaintiff alleged he was injured when a track attendant stopped his go-kart and another kart struck him, and after his counsel sent a preservation letter requesting surveillance footage, the facility's manager investigated, saved footage of the final two laps, and allowed the remaining video to be destroyed. The trial court found the letter gave the operator actual notice that litigation was contemplated but then held that no preservation duty arose because the letter was overbroad, vague, and lacked details about the incident, relying on Cooper Tire & Rubber Co. v. Koch. The Court of Appeals held that Koch addresses when a plaintiff's own duty to preserve arises based on anticipation of litigation, not the sufficiency of a preservation letter's contents, and that no authority supports a reasonableness standard applied to the substance of such a letter; once actual notice of contemplated litigation is found, the duty to preserve attaches. Because the trial court applied an erroneous legal theory, the denial of the spoliation motion was vacated, and the grant of summary judgment, which was influenced by the spoliation ruling, was likewise vacated for reconsideration.

Why it matters: The decision clarifies that the duty to preserve evidence turns on whether the party in control had actual or constructive notice that litigation was reasonably foreseeable, and that the breadth or lack of detail in a preservation letter is not a separate test that defeats that duty once notice is established.

procedure

On cross appeal, the facility operator challenged the sua sponte dismissal of its breach-of-contract counterclaim, which the trial court based on waiver of the contract's alternative dispute resolution provision. The Court of Appeals affirmed that portion of the order, agreeing that the operator waived any right to arbitrate or mediate by asserting a multicount counterclaim, engaging in discovery, and failing to invoke the right to arbitration.

Why it matters: The holding reaffirms that a party asserting affirmative counterclaims and participating in discovery without invoking a contractual arbitration or mediation clause waives that right through conduct inconsistent with it.

procedure

The Court of Appeals also noted that the counterclaim rested on an additional ground not addressed by the trial court, namely the agreement's covenant not to sue and indemnification provision. Because the dismissal order spoke only to waiver of alternative dispute resolution, the court vacated that part of the ruling and remanded for the trial court to consider the alternative contractual ground when it reconsiders summary judgment.

Why it matters: It confirms that a dismissal resting on only one basis for a counterclaim cannot stand as to independent contractual grounds the trial court never reached.
