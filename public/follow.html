Follow the watch

// three ways to get it: your inbox, your reader, or your model

Georgia Appellate Watch tracks new decisions from the Georgia appellate courts, the Eleventh Circuit, and the U.S. Supreme Court, plus Florida and Alabama, filtered for civil litigation and insurance practice. Everything below carries the same content. Pick whichever fits how you work.

by email

Weekly digest

for reading

A short email linking each new decision, with a one-line note on why it matters. Pick the practice areas you want, or take everything. Double opt-in, one-click unsubscribe, no tracking pixels.

by feed reader

RSS

for watching

Standard RSS. No account, nothing to confirm. Three separate feeds so you can take only the one you want.

opinions.xml changes.xml legislation.xml

opinions is new decisions. changes is the one to watch if you cite from here: it carries adverse-treatment flags raised on decisions already published. legislation covers Georgia bills and federal rulemaking.

by model

MCP server

for automating

A public, read-only Model Context Protocol endpoint. Point your own assistant at it and ask what has changed since a date you choose — no key, no account, nothing stored about you.

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "horowitz-law": {
      "type": "http",
      "url": "https://horowitz.law/mcp"
    }
  }
}

Drop that into your client's MCP configuration. In Claude Code it is one line:

claude mcp add --transport http horowitz-law https://horowitz.law/mcp

Three tools:

  • hlaw_whats_new — what changed since your date, filtered to the practice areas you name. Includes decisions published earlier that have since been flagged.
  • hlaw_check_authorities — hand it the cases you are relying on; it reports which carry an adverse-treatment flag here.
  • hlaw_feed_status — whether the pipeline is current, and how many decisions sit behind each practice area.
Read the health field before trusting a quiet answer. Every response says whether the pipeline has run recently. If it has not, an empty result means the feed cannot vouch for the silence — not that nothing happened.
An unflagged case is not a cleared case. The treatment sweep is incomplete and covers only what is published here. Absence of a flag is not a statement that a decision remains good law. Use a citator.

what you are getting

Summaries are AI-drafted from the published opinions and reviewed before they go up. They are a reading aid, not primary law and not legal advice. Every entry links the decision itself — verify there before relying on anything here.
